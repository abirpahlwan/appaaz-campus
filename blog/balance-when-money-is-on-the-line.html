Ludo Club at 5M+ daily players: what scale teaches you

Ludo Club · multiplayer · ~5 min read

Ludo takes about four seconds to explain. Running it live for millions of daily players is a different game entirely — and it taught me how production multiplayer really works.

Ludo Club is the online multiplayer version of the hit dice game. I worked on it at Ulka Games Ltd / Moonfrog Labs (Jun 2020 — Jul 2022): Node.js micro-services on the backend, Unity3D and Cocos frontends, real-time multiplayer synchronisation, and live operations.

Micro-services, because one server can't hold millions of rooms

Rooms, matches and player state are spread across services rather than living in one process. That keeps a spike in one region from taking down everyone, and lets each piece — matchmaking, match state, accounts — scale and deploy on its own rhythm.

Sync is the product

In a live dice match, the client and the server will disagree: a dropped connection, a late packet, a player who leaves mid-match and comes back. The server has to stay authoritative about turns and state, and reconciliation has to be invisible to the players still at the table. None of that shows up in a screenshot, and all of it decides whether the game is playable.

Production debugging is a different discipline

The bug is in someone else's match, on a device you don't have, on a connection you can't reproduce — and the game keeps running while you look for it. You learn to reason from logs and state rather than from a breakpoint, and to ship retention fixes into a codebase where anything you break is broken for everyone at once.

Simple rules, hard infrastructure. That combination is why this project stays on my portfolio.

Ludo Club is live at ludoclub.com. I'm a Software Engineer at Metamorphosis Ltd — more of what I've built is in the village.
