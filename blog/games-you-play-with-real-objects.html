AR that has to work in someone's living room

Dreamerz Lab & Activation · AR/VR · ~5 min read

A demo that works in the office is not an app. AR has to survive low-end phones, bad light, small rooms — and users who have never placed a virtual sofa before.

The catalogue

  • AR Furniture Placement (Dreamerz Lab, 2019) — real-time visualisation with ARCore and Unity3D: plane detection, 3D model rendering and mobile optimisation.
  • Live Art (Activation, 2019–2020) — AR e-commerce for artists and collectors with ARFoundation: product visualisation, marketplace and digital asset management. Live at liveartapp.com.
  • Hand Gesture Recognition (academic, 2020) — deep-learning 3D hand tracking with TensorFlow, MediaPipe and Unity, with VR/AR and mobile AR support.
  • VR Painting App (Dreamerz Lab, 2018) — immersive 3D painting with inertial sensing, real-time tracking and handheld controller localisation.

Tracking is a filter, not a reading

Phones report noisy sensor data. Turning that into a sofa that stays put — or a hand that stays tracked — means filtering, prediction and graceful recovery when tracking is lost. What the software does when something goes wrong is part of the product.

Usability beats fidelity

Nobody keeps an AR app because the shadows are nice. They keep it because placing, moving and buying feel obvious the first time. Simple interfaces for complex tracking features are most of the work.

I'd build another one of these tomorrow.
